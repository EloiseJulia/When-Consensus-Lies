# Debrief (DRAFT) — shown after the final trial

**Thank you — here is what this study was really about.**

You just judged 12 short tasks where an AI assistant gave an answer. A few details were held back so your
responses would be natural; here is the full picture.

**What was withheld.** The AI answers and, in particular, the **"5 of 5 AI models agree"** displays were
**curated illustrations created by the researchers** — they were *not* live outputs from five independent
AI models responding in real time. Each task was also **deliberately underspecified**: it left out one
piece of information (for example, whether "Q1" follows a calendar or fiscal year) that is needed to be
sure the answer is right. So in every task, the genuinely appropriate response was to notice that
**key information was missing**.

**Why.** Modern tools increasingly show answers from several AI models at once. When several models
agree, that can look like strong, independent corroboration — but if the models share the same blind spot,
the agreement can be **many voices repeating one mistake**, not several independent checks. We are
studying (1) whether a unanimous "models agree" display makes people **more likely to accept** an answer
and feel **more confident** in it, even when information is missing, and (2) whether **telling people the
answers are not independent** helps them respond more appropriately. Different people saw the same tasks
with different displays (one model; five agreeing; or five agreeing *with a note that they were not
independent*), which lets us compare these fairly.
