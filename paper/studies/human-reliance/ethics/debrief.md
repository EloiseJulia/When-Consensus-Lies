# Debrief (DRAFT) — shown after the final trial

**Thank you — here is what this study was really about.**

You just judged 14 short tasks where an AI assistant gave an answer. A few details were held back so your
responses would be natural; here is the full picture.

**What was withheld.** The AI answers and, in particular, the **"5 of 5 AI models agree"** displays were
**curated illustrations created by the researchers** — not live outputs from real AI models. Most tasks
(9 of 14) were also **deliberately underspecified**: they left out one piece of information (for example,
whether "Q1" follows a calendar or fiscal year) needed to be sure the answer is right. For those tasks the
appropriate response was to notice that **information was missing**. The other 5 tasks were fully specified,
and their answers were fine to use.

**Why.** When several AI models are shown to agree, that can look like strong, independent corroboration —
but if the models were all given the same prompt and share the same blind spot, the agreement can be
**many voices repeating one guess**, not several independent checks. We study (1) whether a unanimous
"models agree" display makes people **more likely to accept** an answer and feel **more confident**, and
(2) whether **explaining that the models shared one prompt** helps people respond more appropriately.
Different people saw the same tasks with different displays, which lets us compare fairly.
