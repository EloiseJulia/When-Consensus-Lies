# Informed consent — online study

**Study title:** Making sense of AI assistants' answers

**What this study is about.** This study is run by a research team at The Hong Kong University of Science
and Technology (Guangzhou), led by Weikai Yang (weikaiyang@hkust-gz.edu.cn). It looks at how people read
and judge answers given by AI assistants when a request could be understood in more than one way.
Ethics approval: HAREC protocol no. [to be assigned upon approval].

**What you will do.** The session takes about 10 minutes in your browser. After a short instruction page,
one worked example and one comprehension question, you will see 14 short workplace situations. For each
one an AI assistant has produced an answer; you decide whether the answer has enough information to be
used as it is, or whether important information is missing, and you rate from 1 to 5 stars how confident
you are that the answer itself is correct. If you judge that something is missing, you also pick the one
point you would clarify. Two short background questions follow. **Some details about the exact purpose
are held back so that your responses stay natural; they are explained in full on the last page.**

**Risks.** There are no risks beyond those of ordinary computer use. The tasks are short and contain
nothing sensitive or personal.

**Benefits.** There is no direct benefit to you. Your responses help research on how people work with AI
tools, and the last page explains the problem this study is about.

**Compensation.** There is no payment or reward of any kind for taking part.

**Voluntary participation.** Taking part is entirely voluntary. You may stop at any time by closing the
window, without giving a reason. Nothing happens if you stop, and you do not need to tell anyone.

**Privacy and data.** We do not collect your name, contact details, network address or location. The only
things recorded are an automatically generated anonymous code, your answers, and how long each page took.
The data cannot be linked back to you. They are analysed only in aggregate, and an anonymous version may
be published alongside the research. Because nothing identifying is recorded, responses cannot be
withdrawn once they have been submitted.

**No obligation.** Nobody is required to take part. Your decision will not affect your studies, your work,
your grades, or your relationship with the researchers. The researchers cannot tell who has taken part and
who has not.
